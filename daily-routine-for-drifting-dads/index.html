What’s a simple daily routine for married dads who feel the drift?

Keep it to three finite actions a day—one for marriage, one for kids, one for yourself.

Feeling distant at home while still loving your family is common. The fix is rarely a speech. It is a short routine that makes connection the default.

Why evenings drift

You provide. You care. Still, nights can become logistics: short answers, parallel screens, a mind that never left work. That pattern is drift, not proof you are a bad husband or dad.

A routine works when it is finite. Open-ended self-improvement becomes another job at home.

The routine: three actions, then stop

  1. Marriage: ask “How are you, really?” Put the phone down. Listen to the whole answer.
  2. Kids: let one child lead for a little while, or give one short uninterrupted block of attention.
  3. Yourself: take a moment before you walk in, or dock the phone for one declared block so you can arrive as a person.

If energy is low, keep all three under ten minutes each. Done beats dramatic.

How to run it tonight

Do not wait for a better week. Pick the three actions before you get home, or as soon as you walk in. Finish them. Stop. You do not need a fourth category and you do not need to cover every child every night.

Tomorrow you run the same shape with fresh specifics.
